The Left Hand of Darkness
Le Guin

The ice had been quiet all night, and by morning the wind had dropped to nothing. We ate in the tent with the flap open, which we had not done in nine days, and the cold came in like a visitor who means to stay.

Estraven checked the sledge lashings twice. I checked them a third time, which he allowed without comment. That was the shape our friendship had taken: a set of small permissions.

By the time we started the light was flat and shadowless, the kind that makes distance a guess. The Gobrin Ice does not give you horizons. It gives you a white that goes on until it stops being white.

Chapter 7 · The Place Inside the Blizzard

We went on across the plain, and the sky above us was the color of pewter, and nothing moved in it. Estraven walked ahead, pulling, and I came behind.

Once he stopped and stood for a while looking at nothing I could see. When I came up to him he said, “There,” and pointed, and I still saw nothing.

“It is the Ice talking,” he said, and took up the harness again.
